Not All Men

Ek kachauri do samosa; in mardon ka kya bharosa.

Not all men are rapists, although every rapist is a man. Protesting against the mard jaat (men) is a gross generalisation. It is also a recognised logical fallacy called the fallacy of composition. It's an informal logical error which occurs when you assert that if a property \(P\) is true for a subset of \(A\) then it must be true for the whole of \(A\). So the protest against men must be irrational.

But there is more to the story.

The difference comes from what the statistic people call a Type-II error: an error on the failure to recognise a false negative. If a woman labels a nice person as a predator then she has little to lose or gain from it. But if a woman recognises a predator as a nice person, then the outcome can be fatal. So, even though the probability of an arbitrary man turning out to be a predator is extremely low, the infinitely worse consequences outweigh the odds. Therefore, it's a perfectly rational strategy for a woman to mistrust every man.

Not all men are rapists, but how is a woman to know which one are and which aren't?

If you hear a sound in the bushes, you run. If there isn't a loin, you don't miss much. If there is a loin, you are dead. Always running is a much better strategy.

According to NCRB data, in 97.5% of cases registered of sexual assault, the accused is known to the victim. In 43% of the cases, the accused is from family friends, neighbours, and other close people. And these are avoidable child abuse, which are generally done by cousins, relatives, teachers, and other family members. In the evidence of these data, it makes perfect sense to me if women show anger and protest towards all men given the hugely asymmetrical cost.

When can this fear be eradicated? As we have discussed, an infinitely worse consequence will always outweigh a low probability unless it's near zero. Low probability isn't enough, it should be zero. Not all men is already a sign of a bad situation, it should be Not any man (or simply No Man.)

It's also asymmetrical because if the probability is to be brought down, the responsibility lies more on men than women. A woman has fewer chances of knowing if any man is (or has the potential of) being a predator than his male peers, and if there is any chance, only male peers can knock any sense into him. Men being collectively vocal and critical about these things might make the environment safer for women. So, a part of the anger shown is also appealing to men, requesting them to come out.

The fact that men work hard and father bear the responsibility is irrelevant to the question. Working hard doesn't lower anyone's chances of being a predator, neither does being a father. If you scan the list of all convicts of assault, you will find all kinds of people: hardworking, lazy, married, single, fathers, and sons. The case of Atul Subhash and all are also irrelevant; these are examples of whataboutism. They should be dealt with on their own but not used as a tool of distraction from the topic that is being discussed.

Two years ago, I would have stood with a banner of Not All Men. But I have learned much in the last two years. At the end I want to say that these slogans are not literal attacks on men, these are rhetoricals to attack the dominence of men. We don't need to get offended! And there are reasons behind these radical and offence rhetoricals as I have explained above. I hope we understand the true purpose of the protets.

Hasta Proximo. Cheers!
